Alternatives considered For reference

The three options we brainstormed. Final design borrows from A (Neighbourhood chart) and B (4-card grid), dropping Option C (horizon strip) entirely and dropping the "Full field" pill that A originally had.

Today Broken

183 entries, all rendered as overlapping lines. Two gold (your entries) and one mint (leader) are buried in 180 grey threads.

Your entries (2) Leader (1) Everyone else (180)

Bump charts hit a perceptual ceiling at ~15–20 lines. We're ~10× past that. The 180 grey threads carry no extractable signal — they exist purely as texture.

Option A — Neighbourhood focal view Recommended

Default: 7 entries around you + the leader as a ghost reference. Switch the lens with pill controls.

You Leader (ghost) Neighbours
Wins 7 lines is readable on a 375px mobile screen. Answers the only question a leaderboard user has: "Am I catching anyone? Is anyone catching me?". Reuses the existing snapshots endpoint — no new backend.
Loses The "global drama" of seeing the whole pool churn. Power users get it back via the Full field pill, but that's now a click away from the default.

Option B — Story cards Editorial

Replace the single mega-chart with a grid of micro-stories. Each card is a 2-3 line sparkline-bump with a caption that names the drama. "Full field" remains as a clickthrough.

Wins Turns dead data into editorial. Feels like Spotify Wrapped for the pool — eminently shareable. Each card answers "what's interesting today?" without requiring the user to read the chart.
Loses Direct comparability ("can I see myself vs. anyone in particular?"). More backend work: each story needs its own derivation query against snapshots.

Option C — Horizon strip Most novel

Abandon line crossings entirely. Each rank-row is a track; each day is a column; each entry is a coloured pill that physically moves between tracks. Your entry pinned to the top of the visible window.

Wins Discrete pills are easier to track with the eye than crossing lines. Scales to 183 because there are no overlapping strokes. Your entry pinned to the top is a strong UX guarantee.
Loses Novel pattern — no off-the-shelf D3/Visx component. ~2-3× more invention vs Option A. Hover-trail tooling needs to be built from scratch.

Decision log

Chose Option A's chart + Option B's full grid · dropped "Full field" pill

The final design combines the strongest parts of both A and B: the focal Neighbourhood chart from A solves the readability problem; the full 4-card grid from B (not the watered-down "one rotating card" I'd originally recommended) gives the page real editorial weight. The "Full field" pill from Option A is gone — the 183-line chart was the problem we're solving, and the Standings tab already lists every entry. Option C (horizon strip) was passed over as too novel for the return.
